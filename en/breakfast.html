Breakfast: The Most Important Meal or Not?

Investigations July 2026

Edited by Marvellino Kelvin P.

Introduction

We often hear that breakfast is the most important meal of the day. However, many people also believe that skipping breakfast has little or no impact on daily life. These differing opinions have led to the growing habit of breakfast skipping. This raises two important questions: Is breakfast really important? If so, what impact does it have on our health? In this article, we examine the scientific evidence behind these claims and explore the role of breakfast in maintaining overall health.


Definition and Brief History

Breakfast is the first meal of the day, typically consumed in the morning to provide the body with essential nutrients and energy at the start of the day. The habit of eating breakfast dates back to Ancient Greece and Ancient Rome, where common breakfast foods included bread, honey, cheese, olive oil, and even wine. (Gibney, et al., 2018)¹. Over time, breakfast traditions evolved alongside cultural and societal changes, resulting in the wide variety of breakfast foods we know today. The practice of eating breakfast has continued into the present as a way to help meet the body's nutritional needs at the beginning of the day.


The Relationship Between Breakfast and Cardiovascular Disease

Breakfast also plays an important role in human health. Numerous studies have examined the relationship between breakfast and various health outcomes, including disease risk, weight management, and cognitive performance. In this section, we compare findings from several of these studies. One such study, conducted by Yu et al. (2023), The habit of breakfast skipping was examined for its association with the risk of cardiovascular disease.(a group of diseases that affect the heart and blood vessels) The results showed that the habit of skipping breakfast may increase blood levels of LDL-C (Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol, a type of "bad" cholesterol that carries cholesterol throughout the body), which may increase the risk of cardiovascular diseases such as stroke and coronary heart disease. However, it should be noted that the relationship between skipping breakfast and these diseases has not yet been supported by sufficient and significant evidence to establish a direct association.² More specifically, it is the quality of breakfast itself that has been associated with cardiovascular disease, indicating that consuming a healthy breakfast may help reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease (Iqbal et al., 2017).³ Nevertheless, the study by Yu et al. (2023) is supported by research conducted by Kubota et al. (2016), which examined the relationship between breakfast habits and the risk of cardiovascular diseases, such as coronary heart disease, cerebral hemorrhage, and stroke, among adults in Japan. The study was conducted through a survey of individuals with these diseases and their breakfast habits. The results showed that regular breakfast consumption may reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease, particularly stroke and cerebral hemorrhage.⁴


The Relationship Between Breakfast and Weight Loss

study by Yu et al. (2023) also suggested that skipping breakfast may serve as an alternative method for weight loss. Although a slight reduction in body weight may occur, this finding is inconsistent with the study by Dhurandhar et al. (2014), which reported that there is no association between weight loss and the habit of skipping breakfast. Therefore, for individuals who wish to lose weight, skipping breakfast is unlikely to have any significant effect.⁵


The Relationship Between Breakfast, Cognitive Function, and Mental Health

Another study conducted by Sliwa et al. (2023) examined the relationship between regular breakfast consumption and academic performance among high school students in the United States. The results showed that students who did not have a regular breakfast routine reported lower academic performance than those who regularly ate breakfast.⁶ The findings are consistent with those of Salsabila and Nareswari (2023), who reported that breakfast has a positive effect on students' concentration. This is because the brain uses most of the energy produced by the body's cells to perform its functions (This energy is derived from complex nutrients in food, such as carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. These nutrients are broken down through catabolism, and simple sugars undergo phosphorylation within cells to produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the body's primary energy source. Simpler forms of proteins and fats also contribute to ATP production.). Therefore, after waking up with depleted glucose stores (an empty stomach), the brain may not function optimally.⁷ In addition, reduced brain and nervous system function may also contribute to poorer mental health. This was further supported by Zhang et al. (2024), who found that the habit of skipping breakfast may increase the risk of developing ADHD, major depression, or reduced concentration.⁸


Conclusion

Overall, breakfast plays an important role in maintaining human health. It may help reduce the risk of cardiovascular diseases, such as stroke, while also supporting mental health and cognitive function. However, breakfast does not appear to have a significant effect on weight loss. Therefore, maintaining a regular and healthy breakfast routine is highly recommended.
